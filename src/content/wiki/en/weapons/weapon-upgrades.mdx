## How do I upgrade weapons?

Upgrade at the **Forge** whenever you rest at a Bell. Each weapon level costs **Remnant Dust plus materials** — the same dust that funds your ability tree — and hits new thresholds that **unlock weapon skills**, extending combos and movesets. With 26 weapons in the game (overview in the [weapons guide](/weapons/getting-started/)), upgrading is how a good weapon becomes *your* weapon.

The upgrade rules apply to **Phantom Edges** as well — the 25 special armaments with traversal and combat powers detailed in the [Phantom Edges guide](/weapons/phantom-edges/).

![A forge workshop with a master smith tempering a curved blade](/images/articles/pbz-upgrades.jpg)

## Can I get my materials back?

Yes — and this is the system's best feature:

- **Refunding a weapon or Phantom Edge is free up to Level 10.** You get back its materials and Remnant Dust in full.
- **Above Level 10, refunds need a Reforging Talisman.** The talisman is a consumable, so high-end commitments are deliberate choices.

This means the entire pre-10 upgrade space is **risk-free experimentation**. Try a polearm for a chapter, refund it, and move your entire investment into a paired blade — you lose nothing but the visit to the Forge.

## How should I split dust between weapons and abilities?

Both systems drink from the same Remnant Dust pool, and enemies stay dead when you rest — the supply is finite. A workable priority for the first half of the game:

1. **Ability tree first, niche weapons later.** The [best early abilities](/guides/best-abilities-first/) apply to every weapon you will ever pick up; a weapon upgrade only boosts one.
2. **One main weapon, upgraded deep.** Pick a moveset that fits you, push it toward the skill thresholds, and keep a second weapon at parity for the swap-refill combo loop.
3. **Phantom Edge levels follow your exploration.** Edges double as keys — red obstacle icons on the map turn green when you hold the Edge that clears them — so their upgrades pay exploration dividends.

## Where do materials come from?

- **Clearing regions.** Each area supplies enough upgrade materials for the weapons it hands you; buying materials at a Bell shop is widely considered a waste of dust.
- **Exploration rewards.** Notes, book pages, and writing on walls pay Remnant Dust and can start side quests whose rewards include materials — worth doing before they expire (see [expiring side quests](/guides/expiring-side-quests/)).
- **Boss rewards.** With 50-plus bosses, most of the arsenal drops from the fights themselves — the game hands you new toys faster than you can fund them, so let drops guide your experiments.

<Callout type="tip">Revert weapons you have stopped using. A refund returns dust you can immediately push into your current main — the economy rewards decisiveness, not hoarding. Accessory slots that carry passive bonuses are covered in [items & resources](/items/getting-started/).</Callout>
