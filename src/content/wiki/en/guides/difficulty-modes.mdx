## What difficulty modes are there?

Three main modes, selectable from the start, plus a fourth unlockable variant:

| Mode | Target player | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wayfarer** | Story-first players | Gentler combat pressure, generous timing windows |
| **Pathbreaker** | The intended experience | Balanced challenge — the standard the game is tuned around |
| **Hellwalker** | Action veterans | Faster, less forgiving enemies that punish repeated moves |
| **Sixty-Six Days** | Mastery runs | Hellwalker rules **plus a 66-life counter — each death costs one life** |

Hellwalker's enemies react more aggressively if you spam the same attacks, and premade combos matter less — expect to engage with the full depth of the combat system covered in the [combat system guide](/guides/combat-system/).

## What is Sixty-Six Days mode?

**Sixty-Six Days** is the post-game roguelike-flavored variant. In-game, its unlock condition reads: *"Hellwalker difficulty, with one added rule: you have only 66 lives. Unlocks after completing Pathbreaker or Hellwalker."*

Key rules:

- Each **death consumes one of the 66 lives**; when the counter hits zero, the run ends.
- A finished run's data becomes history for good — the game asks whether you want to start over.
- The mode has an ending of its own — Undying Grudge — separate from the three story endings (see the [endings guide](/guides/endings-guide/)).
- It is the hardest challenge the game offers; the [boss fundamentals guide](/bosses/getting-started/) is required reading before a serious attempt.

<Callout type="warn">Do not confuse this with the story: Soul's 66 days is **framing only** on a normal playthrough — there is no time limit and no life counter until you explicitly start Sixty-Six Days mode.</Callout>

## Does difficulty lock any content?

No. This is one of Phantom Blade Zero's most player-friendly design choices:

- **All four endings** — Final Battle, Long's Roar, and Annihilation on any mode, plus Undying Grudge inside Sixty-Six Days itself — are available to every player, including the [true ending](/guides/true-ending/).
- **Every boss** — all 50-plus of them — fights the same regardless of difficulty.
- The platinum trophy is achievable in a single playthrough on **any** difficulty, so trophy hunters can play at their comfort level.

Difficulty only changes how demanding the moment-to-moment combat is. If you want the spectacle without the grind, Wayfarer loses nothing but pride; if you want the full wuxia action fantasy, Pathbreaker is the intended tuning.

## Which mode should I pick?

- **New to action games or here for the story:** Wayfarer. The narrative, world, and [ability progression](/guides/abilities-progression/) systems are identical.
- **Comfortable with character action (Bayonetta, Nier, Ninja Gaiden):** Pathbreaker. Reviewers broadly call it demanding but fair.
- **Soulslike veteran chasing mastery:** Hellwalker — and once you clear Pathbreaker or Hellwalker, decide whether your 66 lives are ready.

<Callout type="tip">You can treat the modes as a natural arc: learn the systems on Pathbreaker, then roll a Cycle (New Game+) into Hellwalker for the long-tail abilities — the [New Cycle guide](/guides/new-cycle/) explains what carries over.</Callout>
